    Buying it, and the thing people get wrong afterwards

    1. Fund SOL

      You need SOL in a Solana wallet — Phantom, Solflare, Backpack. SOL is the gas and the deepest route in. Keep ~0.02 SOL spare: a first-time TAO buy opens a token account and that costs rent.

    2. Swap on Jupiter, with slippage set by hand

      Open the prefilled route, then confirm the output mint matches the address above character for character — aggregator search will happily show you five things called TAO. Read the price-impact line before signing: the book is thin, so a size that is nothing in SOL is a real haircut here.

    3. Know what you are holding

      A wrapped claim on TAO, tradeable on Solana, kept near the native price by arbitrage. It does not stake, it does not earn emissions, and it will not show up on Taostats.

    The chokepoint

    One person stands in the door

    Bittensor's base chain cannot run smart contracts, so the only route between Bittensor and Ethereum is the wTAO bridge — roughly $29M of TAO locked behind a closed-source contract operated by a single pseudonymous developer. Redemption is not permissionless. Withdrawals are verified by hand, at a cadence the operator has described as "at least once a day morning/night." No multisig, no public audit.

    That is not an accusation — the bridge works and it holds real money. It is a structure, and you should know it sits between you and your TAO before you assume you can leave on demand.

    Reported by DL News, including the operator's own statements. Wormhole itself does not reach Bittensor — Bittensor is not on its supported-chain list.

    The path that isn't this

    Staking doesn't need the wrapper at all

    Bittensor's EVM layer exposes a staking precompile. The V2 interface takes a netuid, so you can stake into a specific subnet from an ordinary EVM transaction — and addStakeLimit carries a limit price and a partial-fill flag, so you are not staking blind into a moving alpha pool. 

    addStake(bytes32 hotkey, uint256 amount, uint256 netuid)
addStakeLimit(hotkey, amount, limit_price,
              allow_partial, netuid)

    LayerZero and Hyperlane both reach Bittensor EVM from Solana. So if the plan is to stake, the move is to route value across and stake there — not to buy a wrapper and try to walk it home.

    Interface from Opentensor's own evm-bittensor examples. Confirm the precompile is live on mainnet before you rely on it — and know that Bittensor EVM is close to empty, with about $28K of DeFi across one DEX, against $520M on the base chain.
